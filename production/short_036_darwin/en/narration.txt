For five years, this man was presumed dead. Then he walked into a police station, claiming he had lost his memory. But one photograph destroyed his story.

England. Two thousand two. John Darwin paddled out to sea in a kayak and vanished. Rescuers found his wrecked boat. But not John. He was declared dead. His wife collected insurance and other payouts. Their two sons mourned their father.

In December two thousand seven, he returned. But a woman searched online and found a photograph from Panama. John was smiling beside his wife. It had been taken in two thousand six, before his return, while he was still supposed to be dead.

John had staged his own death. For years, he hid close to his family. When visitors arrived, he slipped through a secret door behind a wardrobe into the adjoining property. His wife knew. Later, they planned a new life in Panama. And appeared on a property agency website.

In two thousand eight, both went to prison for more than six years each. But their sons knew nothing. While their parents planned a new life, they believed their father was dead. What is harder to forgive: stolen money, or five years of someone else's grief?
