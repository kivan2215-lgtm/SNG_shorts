During a television shoot, a worker broke a mannequin's arm. He looked inside, then called the police. What was hiding beneath the paint?

California, nineteen seventy-six. A disturbing figure had hung inside a haunted attraction for years. Visitors rode past it. A film crew decided to move it. The arm broke off. Inside was a real human bone. This was a human body.

But the examination revealed something even stranger. The man had died sixty-five years earlier. An old coin and museum tickets were found in his mouth. The clues led to a name long forgotten: Elmer McCurdy.

In nineteen eleven, police shot and killed the robber. An undertaker embalmed his body and put it on display. The remains then passed between owners, museums and traveling shows. Decades later, they ended up in the attraction. By then, people thought he was a wax mannequin.

A broken arm gave the man his name back. In nineteen seventy-seven, Elmer was finally buried in Oklahoma. Dead in nineteen eleven. Buried in nineteen seventy-seven. Sixty-six years between death and a grave.
