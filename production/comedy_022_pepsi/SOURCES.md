# Leonard v. PepsiCo

The Southern District of New York's opinion describes the original commercial, promotion rules, order, correspondence and later versions of the ad. The U.S. test market saw the promotion in 1995–1996. John Leonard sent his order on or about March 27, 1996. He enclosed fifteen original points and a check for $700,008.50 and wrote “1 Harrier Jet” on the form. Extra points cost ten cents each. The jet appeared in the TV commercial at 7,000,000 points and did not appear on the order form or in the catalogue. On or about May 7 the redemption company returned the check and enclosed free product coupons. Subsequent versions of the commercial used 700,000,000 points; “(Just Kidding)” appeared in a later version.

The court granted PepsiCo summary judgment on August 5, 1999. It held, among other reasons, that the commercial was not an offer and that no objective, reasonable viewer would take the jet as an actual offer. Leonard did not pay Pepsi $700,008.50 or receive a jet. The Second Circuit affirmed substantially for the reasons stated by the district court.
