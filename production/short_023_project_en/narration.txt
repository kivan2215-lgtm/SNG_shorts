Police found a dead man inside a house. His younger brother was missing. Eighteen days of searching. Then came a discovery nobody expected.

New York, nineteen forty-seven. Police arrive at the Collyer brothers' mansion. The door is blocked. They climb through a window. Inside, belongings are piled to the ceiling. They find Homer, who was blind, dead among the clutter. But where is Langley, the brother who brought him food?

Workers begin clearing the house. Thousands of books, car parts, fourteen pianos. Narrow tunnels run between the piles. Some contain booby traps. The search continues. Still no sign of the missing brother.

On April eighth, eighteen days after the first discovery, workers find Langley. Dead. Just ten feet from where Homer was found. He had never left.

Police believed Langley was crawling toward his brother with food when he accidentally triggered his own trap. The clutter collapsed on him. He died first. Homer was left without help. Blind and largely immobile, he could not reach food. His death was attributed to starvation and heart disease. Help was only ten feet away. But it could never come.
