Imagine you sell soda. An order comes in. A shirt? A cap? Fuck no. A guy wants to know where his military jet is.

The nineties. In a Pepsi commercial, a kid flies to school in a Harrier. The screen says seven million points. The ad team wrote a joke. John Leonard reached for a calculator.

Here's where it gets fucking beautiful. The rules let you buy extra points. Ten cents each. Why drink a goddamn ocean of soda when you can just write a check?

John raised the money. He mailed fifteen real points and a check for just over seven hundred thousand dollars. He even covered shipping. That's optimism. No fucking plane yet, but the postage is sorted.

Pepsi sent the check back. No jet. A federal judge had to consider whether a reasonable person could take the ad seriously. John lost. But who's funnier: the guy who believed the price tag, or the people who stuck a price tag on a fucking joke?
