She called the police. Someone was coming into her apartment through the bathroom mirror! Sounds impossible? Minutes later, neighbors heard gunshots.

Chicago, nineteen eighty-seven. Fifty-two-year-old Ruthie Mae McCoy lived alone on the eleventh floor. She heard someone breaking through a medicine cabinet behind the wall, and called for help. Police arrived. They knocked. No answer. The apartment key did not fit. And they left.

The next evening, police returned. Again, nobody forced the door open. Someone finally entered on April twenty-fourth, two days after her call. Ruthie was found dead. Inside the bathroom was the explanation for her terrifying words.

Behind the mirrored cabinet was a space containing pipes, connecting neighboring bathrooms. Removing a cabinet in the vacant apartment opened a route through the wall into Ruthie's home. This was a real way inside. Residents had already complained about similar break-ins.

Two suspects went on trial. Both were acquitted. But another question remains. Twice, police stood right outside her door. Help was so close. Why did nobody go inside?
