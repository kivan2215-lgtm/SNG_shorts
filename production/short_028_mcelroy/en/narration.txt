A man was shot dead in broad daylight. Dozens of witnesses stood nearby. But almost everyone told police they had not seen the shooter.

Skidmore, Missouri. Nineteen eighty-one. Ken McElroy had frightened the town for years. He threatened neighbors, intimidated witnesses, and kept avoiding punishment. Then he shot an elderly grocery store owner. The man survived. This time, a jury convicted McElroy. But he was released on bond while he appealed. Soon he returned to the local bar with a gun. And threatened the grocer again.

On July tenth, residents gathered to discuss how to protect themselves. Meanwhile, McElroy and his wife arrived at the bar. The crowd headed there too. Ken walked out and climbed into his pickup. People stood around him. Then shots rang out. McElroy was dead.

His wife said she recognized one of the gunmen. But other witnesses said they had seen nothing. Even the FBI investigated. Yet no charges were filed. Decades passed. The killing remained unsolved.

The town had feared a man the law could not stop. After his death, the law was powerless again. Only now, the town was silent. Was this justice? Or a murder nobody answered for?
