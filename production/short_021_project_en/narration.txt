FBI agents came to Amy's home. Someone had paid to have her killed. She had no idea who wanted her dead. Or how close that person was.

Minnesota, twenty-sixteen. The order was traced to a dark web website. After the warning, the family installed cameras and bought a gun for protection. Then anonymous emails arrived, threatening Amy and pressuring her to end her own life. Otherwise, her loved ones would suffer.

Months later, her husband called police. His wife was dead, with a gun nearby. It looked like suicide. But Amy was right-handed, and the gun was on her left. A powerful drug was found in her system. Investigators suspected the scene had been staged.

Home cameras showed no outsiders entering during the relevant period. Investigators examined her husband's computer. They found a digital clue: a Bitcoin address linked to payment for the murder order. The trail led to someone living right beside Amy.

Her husband, Stephen Allwine. The hitman website was a scam. He paid, but nobody came. So Stephen killed his wife himself. He was convicted of murder. The gun bought to protect Amy from a killer became the weapon that killed her.
