Imagine sitting down for a job interview. First question: what does this court ruling mean for music on the internet? You look at the camera. The camera looks back. Apparently “get the fuck out” isn't one of the benefits.

London, two thousand six. The BBC is expecting two men. Guy Kewney is a tech expert. Guy Goma wants a job. Someone walks up to him. Guy? Yes. And that, ladies and gentlemen, concludes the fucking background check. A surname? Steady on, this isn't Scotland Yard.

They powder his face and clip on a microphone. He still thinks this is a job interview. Then the presenter introduces him by somebody else's name. Live. For one glorious second you can see his soul leave his body, check the contract and come back. Fuck. Apparently we're doing this.

And he answers! Talks about downloading music. The actual expert is in another reception area watching some bloke on television having a go at being him. Imagine showing up on time and finding out you've been replaced by someone who doesn't even fucking work there.

About twenty minutes later, Goma gets his actual interview. He doesn't get the job. Imagine updating that résumé. Works well under pressure? Yes. Got a reference? Switch on the fucking telly.
