A man invented the wheel. Australia, two thousand and one. Mate, on the drive to the patent office, did nothing strike you as fucking familiar? You parked four prototypes outside.

John Keogh was a patent attorney. He wanted to test a new system. He picked an invention where checking for an earlier model would require a bloody archaeologist.

On the application it became a circular transportation facilitation device. A wheel goes on a wheelbarrow. A circular transportation facilitation device goes on an invoice for six hundred dollars an hour.

There was a drawing. A circle with a hole in the middle. Somewhere, a prehistoric potter had missed the deadline to object by six thousand fucking years.

And they granted the bloody patent. First came the formalities. Signature? Lovely. Is it round? Fuck knows, mate. Different department. Novelty needed a separate examination. Until then, good luck suing somebody over a wheelbarrow.

Imagine the invoice for a kid’s bike. Two regular wheels, two training wheels. Dad, are you taking them off because I’m ready? Fuck no, son. We can only afford the two-wheel subscription.

John got an Ig Nobel Prize. What a career. Invent fuck all, file it correctly, and go down in history as the man who literally reinvented the wheel.
