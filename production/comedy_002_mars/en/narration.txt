NASA sent a spacecraft to Mars and lost it because of measurement units. Not aliens, not a black hole, just a fucking mixup with numbers. Reaching another planet was possible, but agreeing on units was apparently the advanced difficulty setting.

The orbiter launched in December nineteen ninety eight to study the Martian climate. Nine months in space, complex calculations and entire teams of specialists. Then it approached Mars in September and contact was lost. The weather forecast had become a forecast of a complete shitshow.

Investigators found an error in data about the small thrusters. One ground program reported impulse in pound force seconds, while another expected newton seconds. They sent the numbers but missed the conversion. Brilliant fucking teamwork: one person counts in bananas while the other assumes this is already physics.

The conversion factor is about four and a half. Those incorrect data gradually pushed the calculated trajectory away from reality. The spacecraft passed too close to Mars and the mission was lost. Imagine crossing space only to fuck up the unit conversion. That is like building a restaurant and then confusing salt with cement.

But this was not just one bad line: investigators also found problems with checks and communication between teams. The mistake walked through quality control like a VIP while somebody held the fucking door. The spacecraft was uncrewed, so nobody died except our faith that smart people check obvious shit.
