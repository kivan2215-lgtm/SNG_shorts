A flight attendant was holding the captain by his legs. The rest of his body was outside the aircraft. More than seventeen thousand feet in the air. They could not pull him back inside. But the crew would not let go.

England. June tenth, nineteen ninety. The plane was flying to Spain. Suddenly, the left cockpit windscreen blew out. Captain Tim Lancaster was forced through the opening. A flight attendant grabbed him. His colleagues came to help.

The copilot descended and searched for an airport. Wind drowned out the radio. The rescuers' hands were going numb. The crew feared that the captain would not survive. But they kept holding on. During the descent, the captain kicked his legs. He was alive. They held him outside for around twenty minutes. Then the plane landed. All eighty-seven people on board survived.

But why had the window blown out? Investigators found a dangerous installation error. The window had recently been replaced. Of its ninety securing bolts, eighty-four were too narrow. By just zero point six six millimeters. The other six were too short. Pressure inside the cockpit pushed the window and its fasteners out.

The report identified both maintenance errors and failures of oversight. A tiny mismatch had endangered dozens of lives. Was this one mechanic's fault? Or a system that allowed the mistake to go unnoticed?
