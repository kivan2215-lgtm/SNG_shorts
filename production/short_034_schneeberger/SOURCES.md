# SNG 034 — John Schneeberger: the sample that deceived investigators

## Checked reporting
1. **CBC News, 27 November 1999**, “Sask. Doctor Sentenced for Rape”, reproduced in the Andrew Vachss / The Zero archive: https://www.vachss.com/help_text/archive/schneeberger.html . This is the version actually read; direct CBC access was unavailable. The report confirms the 1992 assault, another victim, blood drawn on three occasions (1992, 1993, 1996), the concealed tube containing another man's blood, later hair evidence, conviction for sexual assault and obstruction, a six-year sentence, and the first victim's statement that she waited seven years. The video uses the report's title and a short factual paraphrase, not a fabricated court document.
2. **Mail & Guardian / Sapa, 22 June 2004**, “Disgraced SA doctor expelled from Canada”: https://mg.co.za/article/2004-06-22-disgraced-sa-doctor-expelled-from-canada/ . Independent corroboration of the substituted sample, the lip-balm saliva obtained by a private investigator, its DNA match, the sentence and subsequent deportation. Later deportation is outside this short's scope.
3. **Lona Manning, Crime Magazine, 3 April 2003**, “Rapist, M.D.”: https://www.crimemagazine.com/rapist-md-0 . Detailed retrospective for two negative tests, closure in 1994, and the private investigation. The article contains reconstructed dialogue and graphic narrative; neither is copied into this film. It is a secondary source, not a police file.
4. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Schneeberger was used to find reporting, not as the sole evidence for allegations.

## Chronology and limits
- The opening presents the accusation and two nonmatches, then promises an explanation without revealing the tube until the last third. A DNA nonmatch is not called a judicial acquittal.
- Lip-balm evidence did not instantly produce the conviction. The narration retains the intervening new allegation and further sampling. Some accounts describe the 1996 attempted sample differently; the short makes no claim that all three attempts produced usable negative results.
- The wording “new samples, including hair” is supported by the contemporaneous CBC report. No invented DNA sequence, electropherogram, forensic probability, lab number or document is displayed.
- The final question concerns the actual six-year sentence, not an invented court outcome. Seven years describes the first victim's effort from 1992 to 1999, not prison time.
- The second allegation is shown as a generic statement/file, not a clinic: the sources reviewed do not justify assigning that incident to the pictured examination room.
- No drug name, dose, incision procedure, dimensions, needle placement or practical evasion instructions are provided. The arm is a neutral educational model, without wounds or a staged assault.
