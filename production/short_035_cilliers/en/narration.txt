Her parachute failed. She deployed her reserve. That failed too. More than a thousand meters separated her from the ground. But the most disturbing discovery came after the fall.

England. April twenty fifteen. Victoria was an experienced skydiving instructor. The jump had been arranged shortly after the birth of her child. Instead of a normal descent, she crashed into a ploughed field. Her injuries were devastating. But she survived.

At first, it could have looked like an accident. Then investigators examined the equipment. Both parachutes had been deliberately sabotaged. Police also uncovered another disturbing event. Just a week before the jump, there had been a gas leak at Victoria's home. Investigators connected the two incidents. Someone had already tried to kill her.

But who could get that close? The answer was more terrifying than the fall. Her own husband. A soldier named Emile. He had arranged the jump. And he had sabotaged the equipment. The court found that he wanted a new life with a lover, and an insurance payout after his wife's death to clear his debts.

In twenty eighteen, he was sentenced to life in prison. At least eighteen years before he could be considered for release. Victoria survived. The man she trusted with her life had tried to take it twice. Which is more frightening: falling from the sky, or discovering who sent you into that fall?
