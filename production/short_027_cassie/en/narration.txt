In a school video, Cassie smiles and says, “Hi.” Police would later find the tape buried in a ravine. Other recordings on it would reveal a chilling plan.

Idaho, two thousand six. Sixteen-year-old Cassie was house-sitting for relatives. Two friends joined her and her boyfriend. They watched a movie, then the visitors left. Fifteen minutes later, the power went out. Noises came from downstairs. Her boyfriend called one of the friends. He whispered, claiming he was at a movie theater.

Her boyfriend left, and Cassie was alone. The next day, she did not answer calls. Two days after that evening, relatives found her dead. The friends repeated their movie story to police. But one could not even describe the film.

Then one of the teenagers led investigators to a hidden stash in a ravine. It held knives, masks, and a videotape. On it, the two friends discussed killing Cassie in advance. They had left a door unlocked, returned, and waited until she was alone. Their names were Brian Draper and Torey Adamcik. In two thousand seven, both received life without parole.

Now remember the school video. Cassie smiles at the person holding the camera. She says, “Hi.” Hours later, that person would help kill her. She had no idea. The plan was already in place.
